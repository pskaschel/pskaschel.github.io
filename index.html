About me

I am a psychologist with a second degree in statistics. Since 2026, I have been a doctoral researcher at the Faculty of Psychology at TU Dresden, funded by a doctoral scholarship of the Free State of Saxony. Before that, I worked at Prof. Dr. Jutta Mata's Chair of Health Psychology at the University of Mannheim, where I remain an affiliated researcher. I obtained a degree in psychology (Leiden, Hagen and Würzburg), a second master's degree in survey statistics (Bamberg) and a second bachelor's degree in sociology and philosophy (Tübingen).

My current work is on methods for formal theory development, in particular on what Bayesian fully-specified structural causal models (BaSCMs) can contribute to it. In a BaSCM, a theory is written as a causal graph with explicit equations and prior distributions. The priors are the theory, so the shift from prior to posterior shows how far the data force a revision. A question of particular interest to me is whether formal theories actually improve cumulative theory building. The dissertation is supervised by Prof. Dr. Daniel Leising (TU Dresden) together with Prof. Dr. Denny Borsboom and Dr. Noah van Dongen (University of Amsterdam).

Beyond this project, my research interests include meta-science more broadly and public mental health, in particular the rising burden of mental health problems.
